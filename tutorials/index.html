Understand the basics before you begin · 3 topics
What is ArrangeUs?

ArrangeUs is an app for dance teachers, choreographers, and directors who want a simple way to plan formations and transitions for their performances.

Instead of relying on paper or memory, you can design everything right in the app - move dancers around the stage, test different ideas, and instantly see how your formations connect.

Whether you're creating a small routine or a large-scale production, ArrangeUs gives you the right tools to bring your vision to life. Walk into rehearsals prepared and actually enjoy the planning process.

ArrangeUs is designed to be simple and approachable from day one - but the more you explore, the more it can do. When you're ready to dig deeper, it's capable of some truly amazing things that take your planning process above and beyond.

Trusted and loved worldwide ❤️

Is ArrangeUs free?

Yes - ArrangeUs is free to download and the free plan is genuinely powerful. You can run full choreography projects without spending anything:

  • Create complete choreographies with unlimited formations each
  • Add comments to formations to capture every detail
  • Customise dancer names and colors to match your real cast
  • Preview transitions and experience the full 3D view

When you're ready to go further, ArrangeUs Premium unlocks even more - music sync, custom backgrounds, props, curved paths, and much more.

How does ArrangeUs work?

ArrangeUs is built around a few simple elements:

  • Stage - your main workspace, representing the area where your performance happens. Customize it to match your real venue.
  • Dancers - the main elements you work with. Add them to the stage, move them around, and customize their appearance.
  • Formations - a specific arrangement of dancers on stage. Each formation represents a moment or period in your performance.
  • Transitions - the animated movement between formations. By default, dancers follow straight paths, but you can adjust them with curves for more natural motion.
  • Choreography - your complete project. It holds all formations and transitions, dancers, stage setup, props, notes, and music in one place.

You start by creating a choreography, setting up a stage, and adding your dancers. Then you build your performance formation by formation until everything feels right. Along the way, it's easy to make small adjustments, fine-tune positions, and capture your ideas as they come. Do it at a coffee shop, in the dance studio, or on your way home - it's all possible with ArrangeUs.

Create Your First Choreography

Set up your stage and get started · 3 topics
How do I create my first choreography? Key

Tap the + button on the home screen, give your piece a name, and set the number of dancers. The stage size is automatically adjusted based on the number of dancers you select. You'll then choose how to place dancers for the first formation:

  • Backstage - ArrangeUs places all dancers off-stage automatically and drops you straight into the formation editor.
  • Manual - tap the stage to place dancers one by one. Press Let's Go and any remaining dancers would be moved to the backstage automatically.

Either way, the whole setup takes under a minute and you're ready to start choreographing.

You can also create a new choreography using a predefined list of dancers from your Crew, or reuse the stage setup from an existing choreography - we'll cover this later.

Basic configuration of the stage

We recommend configuring the stage before you start creating formations. If you change the stage size later, all dancer positions will shift proportionally - but they may no longer align perfectly with the grid and could need some manual fixes.

Open Choreography Settings and focus on these three things first:

  • Stage size - set the width and height to match your real venue. As a guideline, 1 unit equals approximately 1 meter. It's best to configure this early if you need an accurate stage layout.
  • Backstage config - adjust the off-stage areas to match how much wing space you actually need.
  • Custom background - if you have a floor plan or venue photo, add it now. You can also take advantage of our standard backgrounds like basktball field or cheerleading mats. Each default background has it's own recommended size, so we got you covered there.

Other stage settings won't affect existing dancer positions, so they can be adjusted at any time without disrupting your work.

Is my work saved automatically?

Yes. ArrangeUs saves your work automatically as you go - there's no save button and nothing to manually confirm. You can close the app or switch away at any point without losing anything.

Bring Your Formations to Life

Arrange dancers, build formations, and see them come to life · 3 topics
How do I work with dancers?

Dancers are set when you create the choreography, but you can add more at any time from the bottom actions bar [Dancers → Add new].

To move a dancer, drag them to any position on the stage - they snap to the grid automatically when you release. If you drag dancer out of stage it will return to it's original position.

Tap a dancer to select them. Once selected, you can edit properties like color, shape, name, or short name. Names are set once per choreography, while colors and shapes can vary between formations, so you can visually highlight different moments. Select multiple dancers to move them together or update their properties at the same time.

If you work with the same group regularly, consider creating a Crew - a reusable roster of dancers. When you create a choreography from a crew you can skip writing all the names again.

How do I create formations? Key

Your choreography starts with one formation already in place - on stage or off stage, depending on what you chose at setup. To add the next one, tap Add Formation in the formations slider at the bottom of the editor.

Each new formation is a copy of the previous one, so all dancers keep their positions, colors and shapes. You only need to move or edit the ones that change somehow.

One key habit: always create a new formation before moving dancers. If you reposition dancers on the current formation instead, you'll overwrite it rather than extend the sequence.

How to watch formation transitions?

To preview a transition, tap any formation in the formations slider at the bottom - dancers will animate from their current positions to the new ones automatically.

You can also navigate between formations by swiping left or right on the stage, or using the Navigation section in the bottom actions bar.

What's Next

Share your work and discover what else ArrangeUs can do · 2 topics
How do I share my work with others?

There are a few ways to share your choreography depending on what the other person needs:

  • ArrangeUs file - share a copy of the full project. The recipient can open it in the app and work on their own version. Changes on either side won't sync - it's a snapshot, not a live connection.
  • PDF export - generates a document with all formations laid out. Useful for sharing with dancers or keeping as a reference.
  • Screen recording - many choreographers record their screen while tapping through formations to share a quick visual overview as a video.

Real-time collaboration - where multiple people can edit the same choreography with synced changes - is currently in the works.

Where to go from here?

  • Stage configurations - all the settings for stage and different display options.

Browse & Navigate

Find and navigate your choreographies quickly · 3 topics
Choreographies list structure

The choreographies list is your home screen. It contains all your active choreographies along with the Archive and Trash folders, a tags filter row, and a search bar.

Each choreography card shows the name, a preview of the last formation, description, attached tags, number of formations and dancers, and the last edit date.

Tapping the button on a card - or long-pressing the card - opens all additional actions including sharing, duplicating, renaming, archiving, deleting, and more.

How does search work on the choreography list?

The search bar filters by choreography name only - it does not search inside formations, dancer names, or descriptions. Type any part of the name and the list narrows in real time.

Search only applies to your active choreographies - items in the Archive or Trash are not included. It can also be combined with tags, so keep in mind that active tag filters narrow results further and can cause your search to return nothing if no choreography matches both.

How do I sort the choreography list?

Tap the Filter button at the top of the list and select the Sort By row to pick a sorting option. Available options are:

  • Last modified - most recently edited first (default)
  • Last opened - most recently viewed first
  • Date created - newest or oldest first
  • Name - alphabetical A to Z or Z to A
  • Formations count - by number of formations in the choreography
  • Dancers count - by number of dancers in the choreography

Each option can be sorted ascending or descending. When a non-default sort is active, the Filter button is highlighted so you can tell at a glance that sorting has been changed. Tap it to open the menu and use the Reset button to quickly go back to the default.

Your preference is remembered and applied automatically each time you open the app.

Keep Your Library Tidy

Archive, delete, and understand your library limits · 3 topics
How many choreographies can I have on the free plan?
The free plan allows up to 6 choreographies, each with an unlimited number of formations. Once you reach the limit, you won't be able to create new ones until you delete some existing ones - or upgrade to Premium with no limits.
What is the Archive and when should I use it?

The Archive is a built-in folder for choreographies you want to keep but no longer need in your main list - finished season pieces, past events, or work you've put on hold. Tap the button on a choreography card and tap Archive to move it there.

You can still open and edit archived choreographies or share them - but extended actions like duplicate and manage tags are not available in the Archive folder. To access those, unarchive first by tapping the button on the card and tapping Unarchive.

What happens when I delete a choreography?

Deleting a choreography moves it to the Trash folder rather than erasing it immediately. Each card in Trash shows the time remaining before permanent deletion — items are automatically removed after 30 days.

To view a choreography's formations you need to restore it first. Open the Trash folder, tap the button on the card, and tap Restore.

To remove a choreography immediately, use the Delete action from the same menu. This is permanent and cannot be undone.

Choreography Actions

Create, copy, and work with multiple choreographies · 4 topics
How do I duplicate a choreography?
Tap the button on a choreography card and tap Duplicate. ArrangeUs creates a complete independent copy — all formations, dancer positions, music link, tags, and description — and appends "Copy" to the name. Changes to the copy never affect the original. This is handy for creating a variation of an existing piece or kickstarting a new season's version of a recurring routine.
Can I reuse a stage setup without copying the choreography?
Yes - use Duplicate Stage action. Tap the button on a choreography card and choose this action to create a new blank choreography that inherits only the stage configuration: size, backstage zones, background image, and other settings. No formations or dancer positions are copied. You then pick a fresh set of dancers for the new piece, which is ideal when you choreograph multiple pieces for the same venue.
Can I perform actions on multiple choreographies at once?

Yes. To enter multi-select mode, tap the button on any choreography card and tap Select. You can also activate it from the Filter button. Then tap cards to build your selection. An action bar appears at the bottom with bulk operations:

  • Manage Tags - attach tags across all selected choreographies at once.
  • Archive - move all selected items to the Archive folder.
  • Share - send all selected choreographies at once.
  • Delete - send all selected items to Trash.

Tap Done to exit multi-select without making changes.

What's the fastest way to start a new choreography?

A few approaches depending on your situation:

  • Use a Crew - if you work with the same group of dancers regularly, create a Crew once with their names. When starting a new choreography, pick the Crew and your roster is ready instantly - no re-entering names.
  • Duplicate Stage - if you're choreographing another piece for the same venue, use Duplicate Stage on an existing choreography. You get a fresh blank piece with the stage already configured - size, background, backstage zones - and just pick your dancers.
  • Combine both - Duplicate Stage and Crews work great together. Reuse the stage from a previous piece and populate it from a Crew - the whole setup takes under a minute.
  • Keep a template choreography - create a "master" piece with your ideal stage setup, preferred starting formation layout, and your usual dancer list. Keep it untouched and duplicate it whenever you start something new. Unlike Duplicate Stage, this preserves your formations too - useful if you always begin from the same starting position.

Organising with Tags

Organise and filter your choreographies with labels · 7 topics

Filtering

What are tags for?

Tags are custom labels you create and attach to choreographies to keep your list organised and easy to filter. As your library grows, scrolling through everything becomes slow - tags let you instantly narrow the list to exactly what you need.

You can attach multiple tags to a single choreography, so a piece can be labelled "🌸 Spring Recital", "👧 Teens", and "🔄 In Progress" all at once - then filter by any combination of those.

A simple trick: start tag names with an emoji. It makes tags instantly recognisable in the filter row without having to read the full label.

How does tag filtering work?

Tag pills are shown in a horizontal, scrollable row below the search bar. Tapping a tag pill activates it, and the list immediately updates to show only choreographies that have that tag attached. The active tag is highlighted. To reset filtering, simply tap the All tag — this will clear any selected tags and show all choreographies again.

Can I filter by multiple tags at once?

By default, only one tag can be active at a time. To filter by multiple tags, open Filter → Tags Selection and switch to one of two strategies:

  • Match any (OR) - shows choreographies that have at least one of the selected tags. Great for broad browsing.
  • Match all (AND) - shows only choreographies that have every selected tag. Ideal for narrowing down to something specific.

Managing

How do I create a new tag?

Tap the + button at the end of the tags row on the home screen. Type a name and tap Save. The tag is immediately available to attach to any choreography.

To rename or delete a tag, long-press its pill in the tags row to open more options.

Tips

Best ideas for naming tags

Tags are free-form, so you can organise them however fits your workflow. Common groupings - with emoji examples:

  • Season / Event - 🌸 Spring Recital, ☀️ Summer Camp, ❄️ Winter Show
  • Project status - 📝 Draft, 🔄 In Progress, ✅ Ready, 📦 Archived
  • Age group - 🐣 Juniors, 👧 Teens, 👩 Adults
  • Dance style - 🩰 Ballet, 🎷 Jazz, 🎤 Hip-Hop, 🌊 Contemporary
  • Venue / Stage - 🎭 Main Stage, 🏠 Studio, 🌳 Outdoor

A few practices that keep tags useful as your library grows:

  • Keep names short and consistent - "🔄 In Progress" is better than "Currently Working On This One".
  • Avoid near-duplicates like "Done" and "Finished" - consolidate into one.
  • You can also combine tags from different groups on a single choreography - so a piece can be "🌸 Spring Recital + 👧 Teens + 🎷 Jazz" all at once, making it easy to find no matter how you filter.
How do I attach or detach a tag?

Tap the button on the right side of a choreography card, select Manage Tags, and tap any tag in the list to attach or detach it. Previously attached tags are already highlighted. Changes apply instantly - no confirmation needed.

You can also select multiple choreographies and attach tags for all of them at the same time.

To detach a tag from all choreographies at once, long-press the tag pill in the filter row and select Detach from all choreographies.

When a tag is deleted it is automatically removed from all choreographies. When importing or restoring from a backup, tags are applied automatically - tags with the same name are merged rather than duplicated.

How do I reorder tags?

Long-press any tag pill and select Reorder Tags. Then hold the pill you want to move and drag it to a new position. When you're happy with the order, tap Done.

The order here determines how tags appear in the filter row - put your most-used ones first so they're always visible. If you have a lot of tags, an expand button will appear to give you more room, which makes reordering easier.

Crews

Save and reuse dancer rosters across choreographies · 6 topics
What is a Crew and what is it for? Key

A Crew is a saved roster of dancers you can reuse when creating new choreographies. Instead of re-entering names, short names, and colors every time, you set up a Crew once and pick it at creation - the whole dancer setup is done instantly. Crews don't sync with choreographies you've already created, so updating a Crew won't affect existing work.

Crews are most valuable when you choreograph repeatedly for the same group of people. Some common patterns:

  • One crew per class or group - keep a separate crew for each dance class, team, or age group you work with, so starting a new piece always takes seconds.
  • Season crew - duplicate a crew at the start of each season, update the roster for any cast changes, and keep the previous season's crew for reference.
  • Shared crew for co-choreographers - export and share the crew file with an assistant or co-director so you both work from the same dancer list.
  • Audition crew - build a temporary crew of all candidates, create choreographies for audition pieces, then archive the crew once casting is final.
How do I create or delete a Crew?

Open the app menu and go to Crews, then tap +. Give the crew a name, then add dancers one by one - each with a full name, short name (1-3 characters shown on stage), and color. Tap Save when done. The crew is now available to select when creating a new choreography. There is no limit on the number of crews or dancers per crew.

To delete a crew, swipe left on it in the Crews list and tap Delete. You'll be asked to confirm. Only the crew profile and its roster are deleted - choreographies previously created from that crew are unaffected and remain in your library intact.

How do I create a Crew from an existing choreography?

Open the choreography, tap Dancers in the bottom actions bar, and tap Save as Crew. Give it a name and confirm. All current dancers - with their names, short names, and colors - are saved as a new reusable crew. This is the fastest way to build a Crew if you've already spent time setting up dancers in a previous choreography.

How do I add, edit, or delete dancers in a Crew?

Open a crew and tap + to add a new dancer. Fill in the full name, short name (1-3 characters shown on stage), and pick a color, then tap Save.

To edit a dancer, tap their row in the crew list and update any field. Changes apply to the crew profile immediately - choreographies already created from this crew are not affected retroactively, since dancer details are copied at creation time.

To delete a dancer, swipe left on their row and tap Delete, or open the dancer and tap Remove from Crew. Deleted dancers are removed from the crew roster but remain in any choreographies they were already part of.

How do I share a Crew with another choreographer?

Open the crew and tap the Share icon in the top-right corner. ArrangeUs generates a crew file you can send via any messaging or email app. The recipient opens the file on their device and ArrangeUs imports the crew - including all dancer names, short names, and colors - into their Crews list. Sharing is one-way and point-in-time: later changes you make to your crew are not pushed to the recipient automatically.

Can I connect two or more crews together?

Connecting crews - so that multiple choreographers can share a single live roster - is planned for a future release. For now, the closest workaround is to share a crew file whenever the roster changes so the other person can re-import the updated version.

Presets

Save formation ideas and references for inspiration · 2 topics
What are Presets?

Presets is a dedicated tab where you can save formation ideas, references, and inspirations. Think of it as a sketchpad - a place to capture interesting layouts you might want to revisit or draw from when working on a choreography.

The Presets feature is still in its early form and will be more deeply integrated into the choreography editor in the future. For now it works as a personal reference library you build and browse at your own pace.

How do I create and use a Preset?

Open the Presets tab and tap + to create a new one. Give it a name, set up the dancer positions on the stage, and save. You can create as many as you need and browse them anytime for reference.

When planning a choreography, open the Presets tab alongside your work to reference saved layouts and get ideas for your next formation.

Working with Your Choreography

Deep dive into the choreography editor and its features · 8 subtopics

General

Core concepts and interface basics for the choreography editor · 5 topics
What gestures work on the stage?

The stage supports a few core gestures:

  • Tap - select a dancer or deselect by tapping empty space.
  • Drag a dancer - move the selected dancer (or the whole selection if multiple are selected).
  • Drag empty space - draw an area selection rectangle to select all dancers within it.
  • Pinch - zoom the stage view in or out. Stage zoom is a display setting only - it doesn't affect dancer positions or the actual stage size.
  • Two-finger drag - pan the stage view when zoomed in.
What are the different states and modes of the editor screen?

The editor operates in a few distinct states depending on what you're doing:

  • Default - no selection, stage is visible, timeline at the bottom. Ready to select dancers or navigate formations.
  • Dancer selected - one or more dancers are highlighted, the actions bar shows options specific to the selection.
  • Music Mode - the waveform timeline replaces the standard formation timeline. Formation blocks are shown on the waveform and can be resized or dragged.
  • Playback - the choreography animates through formations. Editing is disabled during playback; tap anywhere to stop.
  • Previews screen - all formations shown as a thumbnail grid. Tap a card to return to that formation in the editor.
How does undo and redo work?

Tap the ↩ Undo or ↪ Redo buttons in the editor toolbar to step backwards or forwards through your changes. Undo covers dancer moves, formation additions and deletions, property edits, stage configuration changes, and most other editing actions.

The history is per-session - it resets when you close the choreography, so there is no undo across separate editing sessions. Autosave means your last state is always preserved, but once you reopen a choreography, prior history is gone.

How do multiple formation actions work?

By default, any edit you make - moving a dancer, changing a color - affects only the current formation. To propagate a change forward, use Apply to following formations, which appears in the actions bar after certain edits. This carries the change from the current formation to the end of the sequence, leaving earlier formations untouched.

Some actions offer a scope choice when triggered - you can apply them to just the current formation, a selected range, or all formations. The scope prompt appears before the action is committed so you can make the right call each time.

What do the different symbols in the actions bar mean?

The actions bar at the bottom of the editor changes based on what is selected. Common symbols you'll see:

  • ↩ / ↪ - undo and redo.
  • ⇄ Swap - swap positions of two selected dancers.
  • ↔ / ↕ Flip - flip selected dancers horizontally or vertically.
  • ⟳ Rotate - rotate selected dancers around their center.
  • ⤢ Stretch - scale the spacing of selected dancers in or out.
  • ⊙ Select all - select all dancers in the current formation.
  • → Apply forward - propagate the last change to all following formations.
  • ··· More - additional actions for the current selection or formation.

Formations

Create, manage, and organise the formations in your choreography · 12 topics

Navigating & Viewing

How do I switch between formations?

Tap any formation card in the timeline to jump directly to it. You can also swipe left or right on the stage area or use the prev/next arrows to step through formations one at a time.

In Music Mode, tap a formation block in the waveform timeline to select it, or scrub the playhead to land on the formation at that timestamp. The stage updates instantly to show that formation.

What is the formation previews screen?

The previews screen shows all formations as thumbnail cards in a scrollable grid, giving you an overview of the entire choreography at a glance. Tap any card to jump to that formation and start editing. It's useful for finding a specific formation in a long piece, checking the overall visual flow, and performing batch actions like reordering, recoloring, or selecting a range.

How do I zoom out in the previews screen?

Pinch in on the previews screen to reduce thumbnail size and show more formations at once. Zooming out is useful for a bird's-eye view of the whole piece - you can check how colors are distributed, spot repeated patterns, or quickly scan for a specific formation without scrolling through them one by one.

Managing Formations

How do I create a new formation?

Tap the + button in the timeline or previews screen. A new formation is inserted after the currently selected one, copying the dancer positions from it as a starting point. From there you can move dancers into the new arrangement.

How do I duplicate a formation?

Tap the ··· menu on a formation card and select Duplicate. The copy is inserted immediately after the original with identical dancer positions, stage setup, and comments. Useful when the next formation is a small variation of the current one - duplicate first, then adjust.

How do I delete a formation?

Tap the ··· menu on a formation card and select Delete, or swipe left on the card in the timeline. Deletion is immediate - tap Undo right away if you deleted by mistake.

How do I rename a formation?

Tap the formation name on the card to edit it inline. Names like "Verse 1" or "Final pose" help you navigate quickly in a long piece and appear in the previews screen and in PDF exports.

How do I reorder formations?

In the previews screen, long-press a formation card and drag it to a new position. In Music Mode, drag formation blocks along the waveform timeline to reposition them. Reordering also moves the formation's associated transition paths so the routing stays correct.

How do I add a comment to a formation?

Select the formation, then tap Add Comment in the actions area. Type your note and confirm. Comments are shown below the stage while editing and are included in PDF exports. See the Comments subtopic for more on editing and viewing comments.

Organising & Selecting

How do I set a color on a formation and group by color?

Tap the ··· menu on a formation card and select Set Color. Choose a color from the palette - it appears as a colored stripe on the card in the timeline and previews screen.

Color is a lightweight grouping tool. You can give all chorus formations one color and all verse formations another, making the structure of the piece immediately readable at a glance. In the previews screen you can filter by color to show only formations in a specific group.

How do range actions and custom formation selection work?

In the previews screen, long-press any formation card to enter multi-select mode. Tap additional cards to build a custom selection, or drag from the first to the last card to select a contiguous range in one gesture.

With multiple formations selected you can delete, recolor, or apply other batch actions to all of them at once - useful for restructuring a long sequence or applying consistent styling to a whole section.

How does Split Formation work in Music Mode?

Split Formation divides one formation block into two at a point you choose. Both halves start with identical dancer positions - it's the same snapshot split in time. From there you can adjust the duration of each half independently and move dancers in either one without affecting the other.

To split, tap a formation block in the Music Mode timeline and select Split. Drag the split point to where you want the divide, then confirm.

Dancers

Select, style, move, and manage dancers on stage · 26 topics

Dancers Selection

How do I select a dancer?

Tap a dancer on the stage to select them. A selection indicator appears around the dancer and the actions bar at the bottom updates to show options for that dancer.

To add another dancer to the selection, tap them while the first is still selected. To remove a dancer from a multi-selection, tap them again.

How do I select multiple dancers using area selection?

Drag on an empty area of the stage to draw a selection rectangle. Any dancer whose position falls within the rectangle is selected when you lift your finger. Area selection is the fastest way to grab a cluster of nearby dancers in one gesture.

How do I select all dancers at once?

Tap the Select All button in the actions bar, or use the select all option from the stage menu. All dancers in the current formation are selected instantly - useful before applying a bulk action like rotating or flipping the whole group.

How do I select all dancers with the same color or shape?

Select one dancer first, then tap Select Same Color or Select Same Shape from the actions bar. All dancers in the formation matching that color or shape are added to the selection. Useful for targeting a specific group - for example, all lead dancers who share a color - without tapping each one individually.

How do I deselect dancers and reset the selection?

Tap an empty area of the stage to clear the selection entirely. You can also tap a selected dancer individually to remove them from the current selection without affecting the others.

Dancers Styling

How do I change a dancer's color?

Select the dancer, then tap the color swatch in the actions bar to open the color picker. Choose a new color and it is applied immediately. Changes apply to the selected dancer across the entire choreography - not just the current formation. When multiple dancers are selected, the color change applies to all of them at once.

How do I change a dancer's shape?

Select the dancer, then tap the shape icon in the actions bar. A picker shows the available shapes - circle, square, triangle, and others. The new shape applies across the whole choreography. Shapes are useful for distinguishing roles visually when color alone isn't enough.

How do I assign a name and short name to a dancer?

Select the dancer and tap their name label or open the dancer properties panel. The Full name is used in lists, exports, and comments. The Short name (1-3 characters) is what's displayed on the stage icon itself.

Keeping short names consistent - like initials or role codes - makes the stage readable at a glance, especially with larger casts.

How do I control what is displayed inside a dancer's shape?

In the stage display settings, you can choose what each dancer's icon shows:

  • Short name - displays the 1-3 character label. Best when you need to identify individual dancers by name.
  • Index - displays a number assigned to the dancer. Useful for numbered cast lists or role assignments.
  • Solid shape - no label, just the color and shape. Best for a clean visual overview focused on grouping and position rather than identity.
How do I control the visibility of dancer name labels?

The dancer names visibility setting is a global toggle in the stage display settings. When off, name labels (shown below or above the dancer shape) are hidden across the whole choreography. When on, full names are shown alongside each dancer icon. This is separate from the short name inside the shape - you can show one, both, or neither.

How do I adjust the size of dancers on stage?

Open the stage display settings and adjust the dancer size slider. This is a global setting that scales all dancer icons proportionally - it doesn't affect their positions, only how large they appear on screen. Increasing the size can make a crowded stage easier to read; decreasing it gives more room to see spacing and paths.

How do I edit the dancer name label size?

In the stage display settings, there is a separate label size control for the name text shown alongside dancer icons. Adjust it independently from the dancer shape size - useful when you want small icons but readable names, or large icons with minimal text clutter.

Dancers Movement

How do I move dancers on stage?

Tap a dancer to select them, then drag to move. When multiple dancers are selected, dragging any one of them moves the entire selection together, preserving their relative spacing.

Movement only affects the current formation by default. To propagate a position change forward, use Apply to following formations from the actions bar after moving.

What is snap to grid and how do I enable it?

When snap to grid is on, dancers lock to the nearest grid intersection as you drag them. This makes alignment fast and consistent - dancers line up exactly on grid cells without needing to be precise by eye. Toggle it using the snap icon in the editor toolbar. The grid cell size is configurable in stage settings.

What is resnap and how do I use it?

Resnap moves all selected dancers to the nearest grid intersection without you having to drag them manually. It's useful after changing the grid cell size - positions that were on-grid before may no longer align exactly, and resnap corrects them in one tap. Select the dancers you want to realign and tap Resnap from the actions bar.

Is moving a single dancer different from moving multiple dancers?

For a single dancer, dragging moves only that dancer. Their position updates and any transition path to or from that position updates with it.

For multiple dancers, dragging any dancer in the selection moves all selected dancers as a rigid group - their spacing and relative positions are preserved. This is useful for repositioning an entire row or cluster without breaking their alignment.

Quick Positions Actions

How do I swap two dancers' positions?

Select exactly two dancers, then tap Swap in the actions bar. The two dancers exchange positions instantly. This also works across multiple formations when using the apply-forward option, and their transition paths are swapped along with their positions.

How do I rotate a selected group of dancers?

Select the dancers you want to rotate, then tap Rotate in the quick actions panel. Use the slider to set the rotation angle - the group rotates around its collective center point as you drag. This is useful for spinning a circle formation or rotating a line to a diagonal without repositioning each dancer individually.

How do I stretch a selected group of dancers?

Select the dancers, then tap Stretch in the quick actions panel. Drag the slider to scale the group outward or inward from its center - spreading dancers further apart or pulling them closer together proportionally. Useful for tightening a loose cluster or opening up a compact group without losing the relative shape.

How do I flip formation positions?

Select the dancers you want to flip, then use Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical from the quick actions panel. The selected dancers are mirrored around the center of their group. Flipping the full selection mirrors the entire formation layout - useful for creating a mirror-image version of a phrase or correcting stage left/right orientation.

How do clockwise and anti-clockwise swaps work?

Select a group of dancers and tap Rotate Positions Clockwise or Anti-clockwise. This shifts each dancer into the position of the next dancer in the circular order - the dancer at the top moves to the right slot, the right moves to the bottom, and so on. The dancers themselves don't change identity, only their positions are shuffled around the group's center. Useful for cycling through role assignments or creating a rotation pattern across formations.

How do I copy and paste positions?

Select one or more dancers and tap Copy Positions. Navigate to another formation, select the same dancers, and tap Paste Positions. The dancers snap to the copied coordinates.

This is useful for repeating an exact arrangement in a later formation, or for bringing dancers back to a known reference position after a sequence of moves.

How do the Presets actions work in the positions panel?

The Presets section in the positions actions panel shows your saved formation presets. Applying a preset distributes the selected dancers into the preset's positions - ArrangeUs maps the currently selected dancers to the preset slots and places them accordingly.

You can save any current arrangement as a new preset using Save as Preset. Presets are reusable across choreographies, making it quick to return to a standard starting shape like a line, arc, or grid without rebuilding it each time.

Dancers Management

How do I add new dancers to a choreography?

Tap Dancers in the bottom actions bar and tap Add Dancer. Set their name, short name, color, and shape, then confirm. The new dancer is added to all formations from the current one onwards, placed at a default position that you can then move.

You can also add multiple dancers at once by tapping Add Multiple and entering the count - ArrangeUs creates them with auto-assigned names and colors that you can edit afterwards.

How do I delete a dancer?

Tap Dancers in the bottom actions bar and swipe left on the dancer you want to remove, then tap Delete. The dancer is removed from all formations in the choreography. This action cannot be undone, so confirm you no longer need them before deleting.

Transition Paths & Ghost Dancers

Visualise and control how dancers move between formations · 10 topics
How do transitions work? Key

A transition is the movement that happens between two consecutive formations. ArrangeUs draws each dancer's path as a straight line from their position in the current formation to their position in the next one - you can see these paths on the stage as faint lines when a transition is in view.

The duration of each transition - how long dancers have to travel - is set on the formation card and can be adjusted independently for each formation pair. In Music Mode, transition duration is determined by the gap between formation blocks on the waveform timeline.

Understanding Ghosts

What are ghost dancers and future dancer positions?

Ghost dancers are faded overlays of dancer positions from an adjacent formation, shown on the current stage view. They let you see where dancers were in the previous formation and where they will be in the next one - without switching between formations.

This makes it much easier to judge spacing and flow: you can see the full picture of where everyone is coming from and going to while you edit the current formation. Toggle ghosts on or off using the ghost icon in the editor toolbar.

What are transition paths and how do I show them?

Transition paths are lines drawn on the stage showing the exact route each dancer will travel from their current position to their next one. By default the path is a straight line, but it can be curved to represent a more realistic or intentional movement route.

Enable path display using the paths icon in the editor toolbar. Paths are shown per dancer and update live as you move dancers or edit the path shape.

Editing Paths

How do I edit a transition path?

With paths visible, tap a dancer to select them, then tap their path line. Control points appear on the path - drag them to reshape the curve. The path updates live so you can see the new route immediately. Each dancer has an independent path that can be edited separately.

What is a 2-point transition path?

A 2-point path adds two control points to the path instead of one, giving you an S-curve shape. This allows you to model more complex movement routes - like a dancer who loops around an obstacle or takes a curved detour before arriving at their destination.

Switch to a 2-point path by tapping the path and selecting the 2-point option from the path controls that appear.

How do I reset a transition path?

Tap the path to select it, then tap Reset Path in the path controls. This removes any custom curve and returns the path to a straight line between the dancer's start and end positions.

Are there any limits to transition paths?

Paths are visual guides only - they don't enforce physical constraints or prevent dancers from overlapping. A few things to be aware of:

  • Paths exist per transition - each pair of adjacent formations has its own set of paths.
  • Control points can be dragged outside the stage boundary, but the path may look unusual if it extends beyond the visible area.
  • Paths are not exported in PDFs - they are an editing aid only.

Stage Changes & Paths

Does swapping dancers also swap their transition paths?

Yes. When you swap two dancers across multiple formations, their transition paths are swapped along with their positions. The paths follow the dancer, so the movement routes remain associated with the correct performer after the swap.

Does changing the stage size affect transition paths?

Yes. Path control points are stored as proportional coordinates, the same way dancer positions are. When the stage size changes, all control points scale with it - so custom curves are preserved and paths stay visually correct relative to the new stage dimensions.

Does flipping the stage affect transition paths?

Yes. When you flip the stage horizontally or vertically, transition path control points are mirrored along with dancer positions. A curved path that swept left will sweep right after a horizontal flip, keeping the movement route consistent with the new layout.

Music Mode

Add music, set formation timing, and sync your choreography to a track · 14 topics

Track Setup

What is Music Mode and why is it useful?

Music Mode lets you attach a music track to your choreography and sync formations to specific timestamps in the song. Instead of guessing how long each formation should last, you can see the waveform and position each formation exactly where it needs to land relative to the music.

This makes it much easier to plan transitions that hit on beats, build up to a drop, or hold through a slower section. When you play back the choreography, dancers animate in sync with the track - so what you see is close to what you'll experience in a real rehearsal.

How do I add a music track to my choreography? Key

Open your choreography and switch to Music Mode using the toggle in the editor toolbar. Tap Add Music and pick a track from your device's music library. The waveform will appear in the timeline at the bottom once the track is loaded.

Music Mode requires Premium. The track is linked to the choreography - it plays back whenever you preview the piece and is included when you share the file.

How do I change or replace the music track?

In Music Mode, tap the track name or the music settings area in the timeline and select Change Track. Pick a new track from your library. Your existing formation timestamps are kept - so if the new track has the same structure you may only need minor adjustments.

Can I use multiple music tracks or trim a track?

ArrangeUs supports one track per choreography. If your performance uses multiple songs or you need a specific section of a track, you'll need to prepare the audio beforehand - combine or trim it in a separate app like GarageBand, then import the result into ArrangeUs.

For trimming a single track to start from a specific point, you can also adjust the start offset in the music settings so playback begins where you need it.

Timing & Editing

How do I set the duration of a formation or transition?

In Music Mode, drag the edge of a formation block in the timeline to extend or shorten it. The block snaps to the waveform so you can align it precisely to a beat or phrase. You can also tap a formation block and enter an exact duration manually.

Without music, duration can be set directly on each formation card by tapping the time value shown on it.

What is ripple vs non-ripple editing?

When you resize a formation block in the timeline, the behavior depends on the editing mode:

  • Ripple mode - changing the duration of one formation shifts all following formations along the timeline automatically. Useful when you're still adjusting the overall structure and want everything to stay in sync.
  • Non-ripple mode - changing one formation only affects that block. Following formations stay exactly where they are. Useful for fine-tuning a specific moment without disturbing the rest.

Toggle between modes using the ripple icon in the Music Mode toolbar.

Playback

How do I change the music playback speed?

Tap the playback speed indicator in the Music Mode toolbar and select a speed - options include 0.5x, 0.75x, 1x, and faster. Slowing down is useful for rehearsals where dancers are still learning the sequence and need more time to move between positions. The speed change affects playback only and does not alter your formation timestamps.

How do I zoom the timeline in or out?

Pinch in or out on the timeline to change the zoom level. Zooming in stretches the waveform so each second takes up more space - useful for precise editing around a specific beat. Zooming out compresses the view so you can see more of the track at once, which helps when adjusting the overall structure of longer choreographies.

Can I preview the full performance with music?

Yes - tap the play button in Music Mode to run the full playback from the current position. The stage animates through each formation in sync with the track, so you can see how the whole piece flows together. Use the playback speed control to slow it down if you want to review a specific section more carefully.

What audio formats are supported?

ArrangeUs supports the standard audio formats available on iOS - MP3, AAC, M4A, WAV, and AIFF. If a track isn't importing, check the file format first. Files must be stored locally on your device or in iCloud Drive - tracks from streaming services can't be imported directly.

Can I use Apple Music or Spotify tracks?

Streaming tracks from Apple Music and Spotify are DRM-protected, which means third-party apps like ArrangeUs can't access them directly. You'll need a locally stored file instead.

The easiest workaround is to use a track you own (purchased from iTunes or imported from a CD), download a DRM-free version, or use a royalty-free track. If you already own the track, it may be available in your local music library - just make sure it's downloaded to the device, not streaming-only.

Does the music travel with the choreography when I share it?

Yes - when you share a .arrangeus file, the music track is embedded in it. The recipient gets the full choreography with music included and doesn't need to have the track on their device separately.

Keep in mind that embedding audio increases the file size, so tracks over a few minutes can make the shared file noticeably larger.

What happens to the timeline when I delete or insert a formation?

Deleting a formation - the block is removed from the timeline and all following formations shift left to close the gap. Their timestamps update automatically so they stay in the same relative position against each other, but the overall choreography becomes shorter.

Inserting a formation - when you add a new formation between two existing ones, a new block is inserted at that position. Following formations shift right to make room, preserving their timestamps relative to the end of the sequence. The new formation gets a default duration which you can then adjust.

How does the Split Formation action work?

Split Formation divides one formation block into two at a point you choose. The dancer positions in both halves are identical - it's the same snapshot split in time. This is useful when you realise a formation needs to hold longer in one part, or when you want to insert a transition in the middle of what was originally one static moment.

To split, tap a formation block in the timeline and select Split. Drag the split point to where you want the divide, then confirm. You can then adjust the duration of each half independently and move dancers in either one without affecting the other.

How do I sync a formation to a specific timestamp?

Drag a formation block in the timeline until its start edge aligns with the waveform at the moment you want. The timestamp shown on the block updates live as you drag, so you can place it precisely. Zooming in on the timeline first makes fine adjustments easier.

You can also tap a formation block and enter the start time directly. This is useful when you know the exact timestamp - for example, a chorus that always starts at 0:32.

Customise Your Stage

Configure the stage to match your real venue and workflow · 10 topics

Stage Size & Layout

How do I change the stage size?

Open Choreography Settings → Stage and adjust the width and height. As a guideline, 1 unit equals approximately 1 meter - so a 10×8 stage represents a roughly 10m wide by 8m deep performance space.

We recommend setting the stage size before you start placing dancers. If you change it later, existing dancer positions will scale proportionally - but they may no longer align cleanly to the grid and could need some manual adjustments.

What happens to dancer positions when I change the stage size?

Dancer positions are stored as proportional coordinates, so when the stage size changes all positions scale relative to the new dimensions. A dancer at the center stays at the center; a dancer at the left edge stays near the left edge.

However, because positions scale and the grid stays fixed in unit size, dancers may no longer sit exactly on grid intersections after resizing. This is most noticeable if you resize significantly. A quick way to fix this is to select affected dancers and nudge them back onto the nearest grid position.

How do I configure the backstage areas?

Open Choreography Settings → Backstage to control the off-stage zones on each side - top, bottom, left, and right. You can adjust the depth of each zone independently to match the real wing space at your venue.

Dancers placed in backstage zones are considered off-stage and are shown with a dimmed appearance. This makes it easy to plan entrances and exits as part of your formation sequence.

Background

What background options are available?

Open Choreography Settings → Background. You have two options:

  • Standard backgrounds - a set of built-in templates like basketball court, cheerleading mat, and others. Each comes with a recommended stage size so the proportions match automatically.
  • Custom image - import any photo or floor plan from your device. Useful for mapping out a specific venue, theatre stage, or outdoor space. The image fills the stage area and sits behind your dancers and grid.

Grid

How do I show or hide the grid?

Open Choreography Settings → Grid and toggle Show Grid. Hiding the grid gives a cleaner view of the stage - useful when presenting or recording. Dancer snapping to the grid still works regardless of whether the grid is visible.

How do I change the grid cell size?

In Choreography Settings → Grid, adjust the Cell Size value. This controls how large each grid square is in stage units. A smaller cell size gives a finer grid for precise positioning; a larger one gives more breathing room and less visual noise on the stage.

What is the Subdivisions setting?

Subdivisions split each grid cell into a finer set of snap points without drawing extra grid lines. For example, a cell size of 1 with 2 subdivisions lets dancers snap to every 0.5 units, giving you more precise placement while keeping the grid visually clean.

Overlays & Indicators

What are distance markers?

Distance markers are numeric labels shown along the edges of the stage indicating grid unit values. They help you quickly read a dancer's position relative to the stage boundaries - useful when calling out positions in rehearsals ("move to the 3 mark on the left") or when aligning dancers to specific real-world reference points.

What are center markers?

Center markers are visual indicators placed at the midpoint of each side and at the center of the stage. They give you a quick visual reference for symmetry and help when positioning dancers relative to the absolute center of the performance space.

What are scene direction labels and what does the Inverted option do?

Scene direction labels show standard stage directions on the edges of the stage - Upstage, Downstage, Stage Left, and Stage Right. These are the conventional directions used in theatre and dance, where Stage Left is the performer's left when facing the audience.

The Inverted option flips the labels to show the audience's perspective instead - so Left and Right swap. This is useful if you prefer to plan from the audience's point of view rather than the performer's.

How do I flip the stage?

The stage flip option mirrors the entire stage view horizontally or vertically. This is useful when you want to switch between planning from the performer's perspective and planning from the audience's perspective without moving any dancers manually.

Flipping is a display setting only - it doesn't change the underlying dancer coordinates. If you share the choreography, recipients see it in the default orientation and can apply their own flip preference.

Props

Add objects to your stage layout · 9 topics

Basics

What types of props are available?

ArrangeUs has two types of props:

  • Stage props - freestanding objects placed on the stage, like chairs, tables, or set pieces. They stay fixed in position across formations unless you move them.
  • Attachable props - props linked to a specific dancer. They move with that dancer as they transition between formations.
How do I add a prop to the stage?

Tap the Props button in the editor toolbar and select Add Prop. Choose a shape, set a label and color, then confirm. The prop appears on the stage and you can drag it into position. Props are added to the current formation and persist across all following formations by default.

How do I edit a prop?

Tap a prop on the stage to select it, then tap the Edit option in the context menu. You can change its label, color, and shape. To rename or recolor quickly, tap the prop and use the inline controls that appear without opening the full editor.

Working with Props

How do I change a prop's position, size, and rotation?

Drag the prop to reposition it. Use the resize handles on the edges to change its size. Rotate using the rotation handle at the top. For precise control, tap the prop and use the numeric inputs in the property panel to enter exact values.

How do I copy and paste a prop's position?

Tap a prop and select Copy Position. Navigate to the formation where you want to apply it, tap the same prop, and select Paste Position. This is useful for snapping a prop back to a known position after it has been moved in an intermediate formation.

How do I duplicate or delete a prop?

Tap a prop to select it, then use the context menu to Duplicate or Delete. Duplicating creates an identical prop at a slight offset - useful when you need several matching objects. Deleting removes the prop from all formations.

Creative Uses

How can I use props as alignment references?

Place a prop - such as a thin line or rectangle - to mark a fixed point on stage like a spotlight position, a stage edge, or a prop table. Dancers can then be positioned relative to it, making spacing consistent without relying on grid alone. Since stage props don't move between formations, they act as a stable visual anchor throughout the choreography.

Can I use attachable props as custom dancer shapes?

Yes. Attaching a prop to a dancer and setting it to match the dancer's color can effectively give that dancer a custom visual shape on stage - useful for distinguishing roles, representing costumes, or marking specific performers in a complex cast. The prop moves with the dancer automatically, so no extra positioning work is needed.

How do I add a comment to a formation?

Tap the formation card in the timeline to select it, then tap Add Comment in the actions area. A text input appears where you can type your note. Comments are attached to that specific formation and stay with it when you reorder, duplicate, or share the choreography.

How do I edit an existing comment?

Tap the comment text in the comments area to open it for editing. Make your changes and confirm. To delete a comment, open it for editing and clear the text entirely - an empty comment is removed automatically.

How do I view comments?

Comments are shown in a panel below the stage when a formation is selected. Navigate between formations using the timeline and the comment for each one appears automatically. You can scroll the comment panel if the text is long.

What is the comment count label on formation cards?

Formation cards in the timeline show a small comment indicator when that formation has a comment attached. This gives you a quick visual overview of which formations have notes without needing to tap through each one.

Are comments included when I export as PDF?

Yes. When you export a choreography as PDF, comments are included alongside each formation. This makes the PDF useful as a full rehearsal document - dancers and directors can read the notes for each position directly from the export.

What is the difference between compact and full comments view?

Compact view shows a truncated preview of the comment - enough to see there's a note and get the gist without taking up much screen space. Good for everyday editing when you want to keep the focus on the stage.

Full view expands the comment panel to show the complete text. Useful when reading longer notes or when you're in a review or rehearsal context and want everything visible at once. Toggle between them using the expand icon on the comment panel.

Backups

Protect your work and move it across devices · 4 topics
How do backups work? Key

ArrangeUs automatically creates a backup each time you open or close the app. Each backup is a snapshot of your entire library at that moment, stored in your iCloud Drive → ArrangeUs folder - private to your Apple ID and accessible from the Files app. The app keeps a rolling set of recent backups and removes older ones automatically to conserve space.

Three things need to be in place for backups to work:

  • iCloud Drive enabled for ArrangeUs - go to iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → iCloud Drive and make sure ArrangeUs is toggled on.
  • Sufficient iCloud storage - backup files are stored in your iCloud container, so your iCloud plan needs free space.
  • ArrangeUs Premium - the backup feature is available to Premium subscribers only.

Backups are useful for recovering accidentally deleted choreographies, reverting unwanted changes, moving your library to a new or replacement device, and seeding a second device with your existing work.

How do I create a manual backup?

Go to menu → Backups and tap Back Up Now. A new backup file is created immediately and uploaded to iCloud. Useful before making large changes, before a performance, or whenever you want a specific checkpoint you can return to.

Manual backups follow the same rolling retention policy as automatic ones - if you want to keep a backup permanently, export the file from the Files app to a location outside the ArrangeUs iCloud folder.

How do I restore or move choreographies from a backup?

Open menu → Backups and tap any backup in the list. A selection screen shows every choreography in that backup - tap items to check or uncheck them, then tap Import Selected. Only checked items are imported and added as new entries - nothing already on the device is overwritten.

Choreographies already present on the device are hidden from the selection screen (matched by internal ID), so you won't accidentally create duplicates when importing the same backup twice. If you intentionally want a second copy, use the Duplicate action on the choreography list instead.

To move your library to a new device, create a fresh backup on the old device with Back Up Now, then sign into the same Apple ID on the new device and import from the Backups screen.

How do I import a backup file manually or delete old backups?

To import a file manually - if you have a backup stored outside the default iCloud folder, open menu → Backups and tap Import from Files. Navigate to the file in the Files app, select it, and ArrangeUs will load it into the import selection screen.

To delete old backups - swipe left on any entry in the Backups list and tap Delete, or manage files directly in the Files app under iCloud Drive → ArrangeUs. Deletion is permanent and cannot be undone.

How do I restore my purchase? Key

Sign in with the same Apple ID or Google account you used to purchase Premium, open ArrangeUs, and tap Already paid? on the paywall screen. The app will verify your subscription and unlock Premium at no extra charge.

How do I request a refund?

Refund requests are handled by Apple or Google, not inside the app.

  • iOS: Use Apple's reportaproblem.apple.com to request a refund. Apple reviews each request and makes the final decision. You can also check Apple's refund guide for more details.
  • Android: Open Google Play → Profile → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions → ArrangeUs → Request a refund. Google Play refunds are generally easier to get approved within a short window after purchase. See Google's refund guide for more details.

For Android, we also have more direct control over refunds and can help when needed - feel free to contact us if you're having trouble getting one approved.

I paid but can't access Premium features Key

First, try tapping Already paid? on the paywall screen - this tells the app to re-check your subscription status with the App Store or Google Play and usually resolves it right away.

If that doesn't help, reinstall the app and try restoring again. Your data is backed up, so reinstalling is safe - but to be extra cautious, we always recommend doing a manual backup first by sending your ArrangeUs files to cloud storage or a messenger before reinstalling.

If the issue persists after that, contact us and we'll look into it.

Frequently Asked Questions

12 articles

Account & Devices

Is there a way to log in and sync choreographies across devices?
ArrangeUs doesn't have user accounts at the moment. To move choreographies between devices you can use the share or backup features to export and import your files manually. Once the accounts feature launches, cross-device sync - including across iOS and Android - will work automatically.
Can I collaborate on a choreography with a colleague?
Real-time collaboration with synced changes is currently in the works. For now, the best approach is to share an ArrangeUs file with your colleague - they get a full copy they can edit on their end. Changes on either side won't sync automatically.

Subscription

How do I cancel my subscription?
Subscriptions are managed through the App Store or Google Play - ArrangeUs doesn't handle billing directly. To cancel, go to your device's subscription settings and cancel from there. Your access continues until the end of the current billing period.
How do I request a refund?
Refunds are handled by Apple or Google depending on where you purchased. On iOS, request a refund via reportaproblem.apple.com. On Android, use the Google Play refund request process. We're unable to process refunds directly, but feel free to contact us if you need help.
How do I activate my subscription on another device?
Install ArrangeUs on your other device and use the Already paid? option in the app. As long as you're signed in with the same Apple ID or Google account, your subscription will be recognised and activated automatically.

Features

How do I change the music track in a choreography?
Open the choreography, go to the music settings, and select a new track. Your formation timestamps will be preserved - only the audio source changes. This requires a Premium subscription.
How do I export my choreography as a video?
ArrangeUs doesn't have a built-in video export feature. The most common workaround is to use your device's screen recording while playing through the formations. Many choreographers share these recordings with their dancers as a quick visual reference.
Can I add more dancers than the limit?
The free plan supports a limited number of dancers per choreography. Upgrading to Premium raises the limit to 100 dancers. If that's still not enough for your production, feel free to reach out and let us know.
Can I get ArrangeUs on my computer?
ArrangeUs is currently available on iOS and Android only. A desktop version is not available at the moment, but it's something we're considering for the future.
Can I create a custom stage shape?
The stage is always rectangular, but you can import a custom background image - such as a floor plan or venue photo - to represent any shape or layout. This gives you a visual reference while working within the rectangular workspace.

Sharing

How do I share a choreography?
Tap the button on the choreography card and select Share. You can share as an ArrangeUs file (a full copy the recipient can open in the app) or as a PDF with all formations laid out. You can also share from inside the choreography editor.
I shared an ArrangeUs file via Apple Messages but my colleague can't open it

This is a known limitation of Apple Messages - tapping a file directly in the chat doesn't open it in ArrangeUs.

The workaround: open the chat, tap the contact name at the top to open chat details, then scroll down to the Documents section. The ArrangeUs file will appear there and can be opened normally from that view.
